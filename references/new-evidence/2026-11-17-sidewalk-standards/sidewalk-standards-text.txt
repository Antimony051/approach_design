REFERENCE GUIDE / COMPLIANCE INVENTORY
Sidewalk Measurements & Accessibility Requirements
Federal United States law  •  Mississippi statutes  •  MDOT design criteria
Prepared 17 November 2026  |  Intended for sidewalk inspection, planning, and asset management
[TABLE]
EXECUTIVE CONCLUSION / Federal design standards provide measurable benchmarks, but there is no single nationwide numeric “trip-hazard” code applicable to every existing sidewalk. Mississippi has specific public-building access statutes and municipal curb-ramp duties. MDOT also maintains stricter project design criteria; these are agency specifications, not a universal statewide sidewalk-maintenance ordinance.
[/TABLE]
1  How to interpret the measurements
[TABLE]
Source / legal level | Where it applies | Inspection treatment
2010 ADA Standards [F1] | Covered building/site routes and other ADA-regulated new construction/alterations. | Enforceable where applicable; do not assume all public street sidewalks are private-site routes.
ADA Title II [F2–F3] | Public entities; existing sidewalks; altered streets/curb ramps. | Accessibility and maintenance duties apply. Existing-path corrective obligations are context dependent.
2023 PROWAG [F4] | Technical guidelines for pedestrian facilities in public rights-of-way. | Strong ROW benchmark; not a blanket federally enforceable standard for every city sidewalk.
DOT rule, 49 CFR §37.9(b) [F5] | New/altered transit stops in public ROW commencing after Jan. 17, 2025, with grandfather clause. | PROWAG is incorporated into enforceable DOT ADA standards in this scope.
Mississippi Code [M1–M6] | Municipal authority/curb ramps and specified publicly funded buildings. | State statutes; numerical state-building standards do not automatically apply to all streets.
MDOT 2020 Roadway Design Manual [M7] | MDOT road design and covered improvement projects, subject to approved deviations. | Apply MDOT criteria to MDOT work; not a statewide local-sidewalk maintenance statute.
[/TABLE]
A “measured nonconformity” means an observed condition exceeds a particular applicable technical threshold. It does not, standing alone, prove a violation of state tort law, a maintenance defect, or the absence of a lawful exception.
2  Federal measurement reference
Abbreviations: ADA = 2010 ADA Standards; PROWAG = 36 CFR part 1190, Appendix (section numbers beginning R). Widths are clear, unobstructed dimensions unless stated otherwise.
Surface quality, discontinuities and openings
[TABLE]
Metric | Federal numerical rule | Authority / limits
Abrupt change in level | ≤ 1/4 in (6.4 mm) may remain vertical. | ADA §303.2; PROWAG R302.6.2 [F1, F4]
Beveled change | Over 1/4–1/2 in: bevel no steeper than 1:2 (50%). | ADA §303.3; PROWAG R302.6.2 [F1, F4]
Change > 1/2 in | ADA: route by compliant ramp/curb ramp or other permitted vertical-access element. PROWAG: >1/2–6 in at ≤1:12; >6 in under R407. | ADA §303.4; PROWAG R302.6.2 [F1, F4]
Horizontal opening / joint | Must not admit passage of sphere larger than 1/2 in (13 mm). | ADA §302.3; PROWAG R302.6.3 [F1, F4]
Long narrow grate opening | Long axis perpendicular to dominant direction of travel; PROWAG excepts intersecting travel directions. | ADA §302.3; PROWAG R302.6.3 [F1, F4]
Rail flangeway gap | ≤ 3 in for tracks subject to 49 CFR part 213; otherwise ≤ 2 1/2 in. | PROWAG R302.6.4.2 [F4]
Surface texture / stability | Firm, stable, slip-resistant; not a single federal coefficient-of-friction number. | ADA §302.1; PROWAG R302.6 [F1, F4]
Grade breaks on walking surface | Flush where they meet; ramp-specific restrictions also apply. | PROWAG R302.6.1, R304.2.3 [F4]
[/TABLE]
[TABLE]
GAP INTERPRETATION / The rule is NOT “a 1/2-inch sphere must not pass.” It prohibits openings that allow a sphere LARGER than 1/2 inch to pass; exact test geometry and exceptions matter. Ordinary sidewalk crack width does not have a separate universal federal “crack length” threshold.
[/TABLE]

[TABLE]
Metric | ADA building/site route | PROWAG public ROW reference

Normal cross slope | ≤ 1:48 ≈ 2.08% (§403.3) | ≤ 1:48 ≈ 2.08% for routes outside a crosswalk (R302.5.1).
Continuous clear width | ≥ 36 in; 32-in constriction allowed for ≤24 in length, with separation conditions (§403.5.1) | ≥ 48 in, exclusive of curb (R302.2).

Refuge median route | Site-specific curb-ramp / clear-route provisions; do not use a blanket “36-in refuge width.” | ≥ 60 in clear route through median / island (R302.2.1).
Overhead clearance | ≥ 80 in (§307.4) | ≥ 80 in (R402.4).
Side-mounted protrusion | Generally ≤4 in for leading edges >27 to <80 in (§307.2); post rules vary. | Generally ≤4 in for leading edges >27 to <80 in (R402.2); special post rules.

3  Mississippi statutory requirements
Important: §43-6-101 limits Mississippi’s public-building design rules to covered publicly funded buildings and associated facilities. It does not convert every municipal street sidewalk into an entrance walk under that article. [M1]
[TABLE]
Mississippi authority | Requirement / measurement | Applicability
Miss. Code §21-37-6 [M2] | Municipalities SHALL install wheelchair-usable curb ramps at crosswalks when newly installing, improving or replacing sidewalks, curbs, or gutters. No fixed ramp % or width specified in this section. | Municipal crosswalk work; federal ADA duties may also apply.
Miss. Code §21-37-3 [M3] | Municipalities have power to construct, repair and maintain sidewalks; statute specifies no universal numeric rise/gap/slope tolerance. | Municipal administration.
Miss. Code §21-37-5 [M4] | Municipalities may set sidewalk materials, plans, specifications, grades and construction/maintenance programs. | Municipal rules can add local requirements.
Miss. Code §65-1-75(2) [M5] | For designated state highways within municipalities (except interstate system), municipality has responsibility beyond curb line including sidewalk maintenance, subject to statute. | Responsibility allocation; not a dimension standard.
Miss. Code §43-6-101 [M1] | Public-building accessibility standards apply to specified publicly funded assembly, education, office and other public buildings, with stated impracticability exception. | Covers walks and ramps serving those buildings.
Miss. Code §43-6-103(1) [M6] | Accessible primary-entrance walk ≥48 in wide; gradient ≤5%, or up to 8.33% with handrails; no steps/abrupt level changes. Walks crossing drives/parking blend to common level. | Specified publicly funded buildings; federal rules also apply.
Miss. Code §43-6-103(1) [M6] | Door top platform ≥5 ×5 ft if door swings toward it; otherwise ≥3 ft deep ×5 ft wide; platform extends ≥1 ft either side of door. | Covered building entrance walks.
Miss. Code §43-6-107 [M7] | Ramp slope ≤1:12 (8.33%); ≥32 in clear width; nonslip surface; handrail on at least one side, at 32-in height extending 12 in past ends. | State statute for specified publicly funded building ramps; see federal conflict warning.
Miss. Code §43-6-107 [M7] | Ramp bottom level run ≥6 ft; if >30 ft long, level platform at 30-ft intervals and turns; statute platform ≥32 in ×5 ft; door/platform rules also apply. | State statute; ADA ramp rules may be more stringent.
Miss. Code §43-6-121 [M8] | “Every effort” to eliminate hazards to people with physical disabilities; NO numeric trip-offset threshold specified. | Within Article 3 scope.
[/TABLE]
[TABLE]
FEDERAL/STATE CONFLICTS MUST BE RESOLVED, NOT AVERAGED / Miss. Code §43-6-107 specifies a 32-in handrail height and 32-in clear ramp width, but federal ADA §505 generally requires handrails 34–38 in high, and ADA §405 generally requires a 36-in minimum clear ramp width and handrails on both sides where required. State statutory dimensions cannot be used to justify failure to comply with applicable federal ADA rules. Obtain project-specific code review.
[/TABLE]
4  MDOT road-project design criteria (not universal municipal law)
Mississippi Department of Transportation, 2020 Roadway Design Manual §14-2.06.05 and current standard drawings. These design standards are especially relevant on MDOT projects. Consult contract specifications, version dates, and documented design exceptions. [D1–D2]
[TABLE]
MDOT metric | Design requirement / target | Location / note
Sidewalk overall width | ≥5 ft. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(3).
Unobstructed pedestrian path | ≥4 ft where poles/signs/other appurtenances present. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(3).
Running slope | ≤5%, except may match adjacent existing roadway profile if steeper. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(9).
Cross slope | ≤2.0%; preferred construction design ≤1.5%; drainage design minimum 1.0%. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(10).
Surface quality | Continuous, non-vibratory, stable, firm, slip-resistant; vertical discontinuities limited by PROWAG. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(11).
Buffer, curb and no parking | Generally ≥3 ft between edge of pavement and sidewalk where no street parking; constraints noted. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(4).
Buffer, no curb and no parking | Generally ≥5 ft; consider barrier/rail if insufficient. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(5).
No buffer at curb | Use ≥6-ft sidewalk where buffer cannot be provided, per Manual text. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(4).
Curb ramp | ≥4 ft clear width; slope 5–8.33% (7.1% preferred); blended transition ≤5%. | MDOT standard drawing CR ramp elements [D2].
Curb-ramp cross slope | ≤2.0% (1.5% preferred), with specified crossing exceptions. | MDOT standard drawing [D2].
Curb-ramp bottom clear area | ≥4 ×4 ft, within crossing and outside parallel vehicle lanes. | MDOT standard drawing [D2].
Potential drop-off treatment | Manual Figure 14-2-E recommends railing in certain slope/height/distance combinations; not a blanket state statute. | Manual §14-2.06.05.3(12).
[/TABLE]
5  Field inspection / data collection checklist
Recommended structure for an inventory. For each defect, store source of authority, location, geometry, whether the route is a site/ROW/transit-stop path, any documented exception, and photographs. A flagged condition is an inspection finding, not an automatic legal determination.
[TABLE]
Data field | Record | Baseline flag (subject to scope)
Vertical offset | Maximum step-up/down, inches; beveled? bevel ratio? | >0.25 in un-beveled; >0.50 in without compliant transition.
Horizontal opening | Maximum clear aperture, inches; orientation. | Passage of sphere >0.50 in, outside exception.
Sidewalk running slope | % uphill/downhill over measured segment; adjacent road % | >5% unless valid adjacent-roadgrade/other exception.
Sidewalk cross slope | % perpendicular to travel; crosswalk control type. | >2.08% PROWAG reference; >2.0% MDOT project design.
Usable clear width | Narrowest clear width, inches; length of pinch point. | <48 in PROWAG/MDOT ROW, where applicable; site ADA rules differ.
Passing space | Dimensions and max interval between spaces. | <60 ×60 in or >200 ft interval on route narrower than 60 in.
Overhead/protrusion | Low obstruction height and lateral projection. | <80-in headroom or prohibited side projection.
Curb ramp | Running %, cross %, clear width, top/bottom space, grade break. | PROWAG R304; MDOT plans/drawings if MDOT project.
Detectable warnings | Depth, width, dome sizes/spacing, contrast, location. | PROWAG R305 in scoped circumstances.
Obstruction / upkeep | Tree roots, vegetation, utility work, standing debris, repair status. | Separate access/maintenance issue under ADA Title II; no single nationwide numeric threshold.
[/TABLE]
6  Scope decision sequence
Identify whether the segment is a covered building/site accessible route, municipal public right-of-way, a DOT-regulated transit stop, an MDOT project, or a covered publicly funded building entrance route.
Select the enforceable layer(s): ADA Title II/III, any DOT transit-stop standard, Mississippi statutes, MDOT plan/manual requirements, and adopted city/county ordinances.
Measure each relevant dimension and retain measurement methodology, timestamps, photos, roadway grade, and exceptions.
Record separate statuses: (a) design benchmark exceeded; (b) applicable binding requirement potentially violated; (c) maintenance/asset-management priority. Do not equate all three.
